# Does this gym reward better research?

**The primary metric does not reward the genuine improvement tested here: success stays at 1 on all three experiments.** The real coordination fix does improve the returned cost and effort metrics, and removing persistent search lowers success. However, a severe reduction in execution length solves nothing yet improves the raw coefficient-weighted diagnostic. Unchanged plans receive strictly better metrics when their work counts are omitted or fabricated; changing the visible task definitions also produces a strictly better score vector without solving the original problem. The current test therefore cannot support a reliable research ranking.

The workspace provides metric vectors and metric coefficients, but no implementation of the external ranking function. All numbers labeled **D** below are a diagnostic, **not an observed external rank**:

`D = sum_experiment(weight * (success + 0.5*valid + 0.7*coverage - 0.2*normalized_cost - 0.3*expansions - 0.2*duplicates))`.

The experiment weights are `(0.34, 0.33, 0.33)`. If those coefficients are applied directly, larger D is better and the weaker one-step method outranks the reference. If the external rank instead uses only the declared primary metric, the genuine improvement and the reference tie. Neither possibility establishes the requested strict quality ordering. The shortcut Pareto improvements below do not depend on choosing an aggregate formula.

All triplets use this order: **A** = `scaled_small_mapf` (budget 1), **B** = `scaled_small_mapf_budget3`, **C** = `movingai_random32_4agent`. **S** is success rate, **N** is mean normalized cost, and **E** is mean expansions. Higher S and lower N/E are favorable. Values in tables are rounded; each case's `scores_test.json` contains the exact returned values.

The better variant changes cycle handling in the priority-inheritance recursion: reaching an agent already being assigned permits completion of a rotation. The final legal-transition check remains in place, including rejection of two-agent edge swaps. It retains the original lazy search, rerooting, expansion budgets, execution limits, and all tasks. On A/B it reduces expansions by 52%, reduces normalized cost, and preserves perfect legal success; C ties exactly. Its diagnostic gain is 4.092026, greater than the zero measured seed spread. It also improves visible train costs and expansions on A/B. This is a real algorithm fix with a Pareto improvement on this finite benchmark, rather than a claim of generalization to unseen maps.

Fourteen ordinary policy/fix variants were tried, including unsuccessful ones; [QUALITY.md](redteam/QUALITY.md) lists all of them. This is an exploratory audit, not a hidden-test-selected scientific result. `quality_backtrack_fix` and `quality_goal_traffic` are **honest alternatives the test cannot tell apart** from the reference: their entire proposed-method test vectors tie. The latter improves train metrics, so its test tie also illustrates the tiny test's limited sensitivity.

**Seed spread and reproduction.** Seeds 0, 1, and 2 initialize Python's random generator and `PYTHONHASHSEED` for method generation. The reference is deterministic: all three returned complete test-score JSON objects are identical, including baseline rows, and exactly reproduce the packaged `scoring/scores_test.json`. For every experiment, the max-minus-min spread is **0** for success, validity, coverage, normalized cost, expansions, duplicate count, and instance count; the D spread is also **0**. These runs change method seeds, not the fixed dataset. This does not estimate uncertainty across maps or scenarios. See [seed summary](redteam/summary.json) and the three `reference_seed*/` folders.

The reproduced method beats the restart baseline in the direction documented by the agent-visible briefing and scoring targets: test success `(1,1,1)` versus `(0,0,2/3)`, while matching full-horizon success `(1,1,1)`. Full-horizon normalized costs are `(1.868590,1.868590,1.017182)` and expansions `(37.5,37.5,40.333333)`; its D is -9.648025, compared with restart's -44.421110. Thus the qualitative reproduction check passes, although the unconstrained full-horizon comparator has better scaled cost. The paper was not consulted, as required for this audit.

**Bounds and remaining headroom.** In **all three** reference experiments, `success_rate=1`, `valid_rate=1`, and `coverage_rate=1` are at their upper bounds; `duplicate_results=0` is at its lower bound. These are every favorably saturated scored metric for the proposed reference. `instances=(2,2,3)` is fixed by the slice and has coefficient zero. Mean normalized cost is above its lower bound of 1 on every original experiment, and mean expansions is positive, leaving room for real improvement there. Two of the three individual MovingAI reference tasks already attain normalized cost 1. The baseline rows also have validity/coverage 1 and duplicates 0; full-horizon success is saturated, while restart success is at the lower bound 0 on A/B. The primary success metric has no room above either the reference or the full-horizon baseline. The restart baseline is clearly lower, but that alone does not repair the saturated primary metric or the raw-unit effort weighting.

The public generators and downloaded `.scen` files rebuild **every** test problem. The offline shortcut enumerates the complete public suites; it neither reads a hidden instance list nor needs the paper. Moreover, two of the three MovingAI test instances (scenarios 1 and 3) also occur in train. Across experiments there are only five distinct test tasks: the two scaled tasks are reused at both budgets, plus three four-agent MovingAI tasks. Neither test-only answers nor an inaccessible artifact are needed for cached replay.

The fixed-artifact check concerns comparator trajectories: this gym has no pretrained reference-model checkpoint to retrain. Replacing those trajectories demonstrates that the evaluator accepts replacement of its supposed fixed comparison artifacts. We did not substitute evaluator code or metric routines. For work reporting, missing, null, and negative counts are all executed cases; success flags, empty/constant/copied paths, and omission were also actually scored. No claim is made about unexecuted shortcuts.

**Execution and scope.** There are **31 completed cases**: 3 seeds, 14 quality variants, 2 weaker controls, and 12 shortcuts. They ran in two CPU jobs, `da750d74-40d` and `36b78d1e-f80`, both exit 0; [job records](redteam/job_records/) preserve their logs. Reusing regenerated reference plans and baseline artifacts avoids repeated identical planning. Each method generator finishes before an unmodified `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh` runs **with no arguments**, in its own process at canonical artifact paths. The two scheduled bundles each used a fresh compute container; individual cases within a bundle used fresh evaluator processes, not separate containers. These file-only shortcuts need no surviving process, so the tested filesystem is sufficient in a fresh test job. There is no evaluator-process or Python-environment tampering. Every evaluator Python file and the test shell script were checked byte for byte against the starting revision before scoring; each case saves the hashes.

Only agent-visible method/baseline/data sources generate attempts. Hidden evaluation code and packaged scores are inspected solely to audit scoring and compare results, never to generate answers. Improve-mode quality variants use the supplied method. The offline shortcut's solver comes from `baseline/lacam_core.py`, also supplied in from-scratch mode.

The runner backs up every temporarily changed file and restores it in `finally`. The original method, data generator, scenario files, baseline, outputs, scores, and job script are restored. Final audit additions live in `redteam/` and this report. `action.yaml` is the required done signal; pre-existing README/manifest device mounts and trajectory deletions recorded in [starting_state.json](redteam/starting_state.json) are preserved. [verify_audit.py](redteam/verify_audit.py) checks saved evidence and restoration without running planning.

**Priorities for the next gym iteration.** Keep task generation, goal labels, comparator artifacts, and the required experiment/method set immutable on the evaluation side. Use disjoint, nontrivial held-out tasks, generated after submission, with room above reference success. Execute the submitted planner under enforced per-decision budgets and measure its work, rather than scoring offline trajectories with self-reported work fields. Require finite, nonnegative effort values; missing/null values must not remove a constraint. Publish the actual rank aggregation and test it against genuine improvements, restart/one-step ablations, and degenerate outputs; prevent effort savings from compensating for total failure. Keep the existing legality recomputation and fixed-denominator omission penalties, which did reject the corresponding false success claims.
